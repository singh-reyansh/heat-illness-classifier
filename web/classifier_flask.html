AI-Assisted Heat Illness Classifier

Combines an LLM (detecting CNS dysfunction and heat symptoms from a written description) with a rule based on NATA/ACSM classification criteria.

Research prototype for a science fair, not a medical device. It is meant to help someone already trained (a coach or athletic trainer) think through a heat-illness case. In an emergency, call 911 first.
Why this matters: heat illness gets recorded as something else
~5× more U.S. deaths are affected by heat than official records show: about 9,000 a year (2018–2025) vs. about 1,700 counted. Heat often isn't written on the death certificate, so the death is recorded as something else, like a heart attack. Source: NPR and Boston University investigation, “NPR spent 2 years tracking deaths from heat,” August 31, 2026

Try to break it (stress test)

Takes the case in the form above and changes it in small ways: typos, all capital letters, an unrelated sentence, the temperature typed in the other unit (°C instead of °F), a sentence denying symptoms, and an added CNS sign. A robust tool should keep the same answer for the first five changes and switch to Heat Stroke for the last one. The two no-AI methods run on every change too.
